Before the Next Heatwave: How Heat Can Affect Your Dog’s Digestion and Behaviour

23 Jul 2026

 

Most dog owners know that hot weather can make dogs pant more or look for a shady spot to rest. But fewer realise that heat can also affect digestion, appetite and even behaviour.

During a heatwave, you may notice your dog eating less, developing loose stools, vomiting, or simply seeming quieter than usual. These changes aren't always separate problems. They're often connected by one thing: how the body responds to heat.

Understanding these changes can help you recognise early signs of stress and support your dog's health before more serious problems develop.

Why Does Hot Weather Affect Digestion?

Unlike people, dogs can't sweat through most of their skin (they only sweat a little through their paw pads). Instead, they rely mainly on panting to release excess body heat. Panting is much less efficient than sweating, which is one reason dogs can struggle to cope during periods of extreme heat.

To help cool the body, blood is redirected away from organs such as the digestive tract towards the skin, tongue, and other areas involved in heat dissipation. This helps support cooling through panting and the body's extremities, but it also means the digestive system temporarily receives less oxygen and fewer nutrients.

When the body is focused on keeping your dog cool, digestion temporarily becomes a lower priority.

For many healthy dogs, this isn't a problem. However, during prolonged hot weather or in dogs that are already vulnerable, this reduction in blood flow can place extra stress on the gut.

Digestion may slow down, the balance of beneficial bacteria living in the intestines (the gut microbiome) can be disrupted, and the protective lining of the intestine may become less effective.

Researchers have shown that heat stress can weaken the tiny proteins that hold the intestinal lining together. This can increase intestinal permeability—sometimes called a "leaky gut." When this protective barrier becomes less effective, bacteria and toxins are more likely to cross into the body, contributing to inflammation and increasing the risk of digestive upset.

At the same time, dehydration makes digestion even more challenging. Water is essential for breaking down food and helping it move smoothly through the digestive tract. When dogs become dehydrated, digestion slows further, increasing the likelihood of constipation, bloating or discomfort.

Which Dogs Are at Greater Risk?

The following dogs may need extra support and closer monitoring during periods of hot weather:

  • Senior dogs
  • Puppies and young dogs
  • Flat-faced (brachycephalic) breeds
  • Overweight or obese dogs
  • Underweight dogs
  • Dogs with underlying medical conditions
  • Dogs with digestive disorders

Ways to Help Your Dog Cope with Hot Weather

Although we can't control the weather, we can reduce some of the extra stress placed on your dog's digestive system.

Keep Your Dog Well Hydrated

Hydration is one of the most important ways to support digestion during a heatwave.

Offer fresh, filtered water whenever possible and replace it at least twice a day to encourage drinking. Some dogs drink more readily when several water bowls are available around the house or garden. Adding a few ice cubes can also help keep the water cooler for longer.

If your dog eats kibble, adding a little water before feeding increases the moisture content of the meal, supports hydration, and can make food more appetising if their appetite has dropped.

Offer Smaller, More Frequent Meals

Since blood flow to the gut is already reduced during hot weather, smaller meals spread throughout the day may be gentler to digest than large ones.

Many dogs also naturally eat less in the heat, so don't be surprised if their appetite changes slightly for a day or two.

Be Extra Careful with Food Safety

Warm temperatures allow bacteria to multiply much more quickly.

Don't leave food sitting out for long periods, especially outdoors. Wash food bowls regularly and store food correctly.

If you feed a commercial raw diet, remember that hot weather increases the importance of careful handling and storage. Because heat may temporarily weaken the protective barrier of the gut, the risk of bacterial infection is higher than usual. Choosing products manufactured using High Pressure Processing (HPP) may help reduce bacterial contamination, although it does not completely eliminate risk.

Good food hygiene becomes even more important during the summer months.

Avoid Sudden Diet Changes

Whenever you're introducing a new food, do so gradually to reduce unnecessary stress on the digestive system.

As a general guide:

  • Healthy adult dogs often transition well over around five days.
  • Dogs with sensitive stomachs or existing digestive conditions may benefit from a slower transition of seven days or longer.

Every dog is different, so the pace should always be adjusted to the individual.

Remember: Behaviour Often Reflects Health

One of the most common misunderstandings I see is assuming a dog has become lazy or stubborn.

In reality, behaviour is closely connected to physical health. A dog that doesn't want to play, train or go for a long walk may simply be trying to protect themselves from overheating. By listening to these subtle changes, we can make decisions that support both their physical health and emotional wellbeing.

Studies looking at owners' understanding of heat-related illness found that many guardians worry that keeping their dog indoors during hot weather will lead to boredom or the development of unwanted behaviours.

Mental enrichment can be especially valuable during hot weather. Activities such as:

  • Sniffing games
  • Food puzzles
  • Lick mats
  • Gentle training sessions indoors
  • Chewing activities
  • Searching for treats around the house

Can provide stimulation without placing additional physical stress on the body.

The key is balance: respecting your dog's need for enrichment while also respecting their body's need to recover from heat.

When Is It an Emergency?

While mild digestive changes can happen during hot weather, heat-related illness can quickly become life-threatening.

Seek veterinary attention immediately if your dog develops:

  • Excessive or continuous panting that doesn't improve
  • Collapse or weakness
  • Confusion or difficulty responding
  • Repeated vomiting or severe diarrhoea
  • Pale or bright red gums
  • Difficulty walking
  • Seizures

If you are concerned that your dog may be experiencing heat-related illness, seek veterinary advice immediately.

Final Thoughts

As heatwaves become more common, understanding how hot weather affects your dog's body is becoming increasingly important.

Heat doesn't just make dogs feel uncomfortable. It changes how blood flows through the body, affects digestion, influences the gut microbiome, and can even alter behaviour.

One of the reasons I'm so passionate about both canine nutrition and behaviour is that they are deeply connected. We often think of digestion, health and behaviour as separate issues, but the body doesn't work that way. During a heatwave, changes in digestion can influence how a dog feels, behaves and copes with everyday life. That's why I always encourage looking at the whole dog rather than focusing on a single symptom.

You don't need to overhaul your dog's routine this summer. A little more water, a little more patience, and a little less pressure to "keep up normal life" can make a real difference to how your dog copes with the heat.

The next time your dog seems quieter, eats a little less, or turns down a walk on a hot day, you'll understand what's really going on—their body is asking for a bit of rest.
